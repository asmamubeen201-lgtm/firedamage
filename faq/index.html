Cost

How much does fire damage restoration cost?

The national average is $27,477, with a typical range of $3,083–$52,031 — about $4–$7 per square foot (Angi, September 2026). Small contained fires cost far less than whole-house fires with smoke and water damage. See the full cost guide for per-service ranges.

How much does smoke damage cleanup cost?

Smoke damage cleanup runs $200–$1,200 per room (Angi, July 2026), and heavy soot can add $2,000–$6,000. Ozone treatment runs $200–$800 and thermal fogging $200–$600 per area; a whole-house odor job can reach $1,500–$5,000.

Why do fire damage quotes vary so much?

Four things drive the price: the size of the affected area, the smoke type (protein smoke is hardest to remove), water damage left by firefighting, and how many contents need cleaning or storage. A contained kitchen fire and a whole-house fire are different jobs entirely.

Insurance

Will my homeowner's insurance cover fire damage?

Most standard HO-3 homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental fire damage, including smoke damage and water damage from firefighting. Policies often exclude gradual damage or damage you knew about and ignored. Act fast, document everything, and contact your insurer before remediation begins.

How fast do I need to notify my insurer after a fire?

Most policies require prompt notice — call your insurer within 24 to 48 hours of the fire. Waiting can complicate or reduce your claim. Call your insurer first, then call a restoration pro to start emergency mitigation.

Can I choose my own fire damage restoration contractor?

Yes. In Texas you have the right to choose your own restoration contractor — your insurer cannot force you to use their preferred vendor. Rules vary by state, so verify with your state's Department of Insurance, but never let an adjuster pick your contractor for you.

Process

What happens during fire damage restoration?

The standard sequence is: emergency board-up and roof tarping, water extraction from firefighting, soot and smoke cleanup, odor removal, contents pack-out and cleaning, then rebuild. You approve a flat written scope before any work begins — no hourly surprises.

What should I do first after a house fire?

First, wait for the fire department to clear the home — do not re-enter until it is safe. Then call your insurer within 24–48 hours, photograph and video every room before cleanup, and call a restoration pro the same day to board up and tarp the property.

How do I avoid fire damage restoration scams?

Watch for five red flags: full payment demanded upfront, no license or insurance proof, high-pressure Assignment of Benefits signing, no written scope, and door-knockers right after the fire. Always get proof of insurance and a written scope with prices before any work starts.

Safety

Is it safe to stay in my house after a fire?

Usually not until a professional clears it. Soot and smoke residue are hazardous to breathe, and structural or electrical damage may not be visible. If your home is uninhabitable, ALE (Additional Living Expenses) coverage usually reimburses temporary housing and meals — keep every receipt.

Is soot dangerous to breathe?

Yes. Soot is fine, acidic particulate that irritates lungs and skin and corrodes surfaces and electronics — corrosion on electronics starts within hours of exposure. Do not dry-dust soot or run the HVAC before professional cleaning, or you will spread it through the whole house.

Timing

How long does fire damage restoration take?

Board-up and tarping are usually same-day. Soot and odor work takes 1–3 days, contents cleaning 3–7 days, and a contained kitchen fire 3–10 days. Commercial jobs take weeks and are phased to reopen fast. Whole-house rebuilds run longer depending on structural damage.

How fast can someone board up my house after a fire?

Board-up is usually same-day, 2–4 hours on site, and costs $300–$900. It is the first call after a fire because every hour an open structure sits exposed, rain, animals, and intruders add damage your insurer may not cover.
