What It Is — How Commercial Fire Damage Works

Commercial restoration is phased: stabilize, document, clean, reopen — in that order, and fast.

  1. Emergency stabilizationThe property is secured, water is extracted, and hazards are controlled so the building is safe to enter.

Quick Answers

How fast can my business reopen after a fire?

It depends on the damage. Phased restoration reopens the least-damaged areas first — sometimes within days — while the worst areas are still being restored. A full reopening takes weeks for major fires.

What is a business-interruption claim?

It is the part of your commercial policy that covers lost income while your business is closed or partially closed. It needs detailed documentation of the damage, the closure, and the lost revenue — which is why commercial crews document everything.

Do commercial buildings have different rules than homes?

Yes. Commercial properties face stricter building codes, safety inspections, and compliance requirements before they can reopen. Restoration crews coordinate with inspectors and code officials as part of the job.

What happens to my inventory and equipment?

Inventory and equipment are assessed, cleaned, or documented as a loss. Anything that can be saved is cleaned and tested; anything that cannot is documented for your claim.

Can restoration happen while part of my business stays open?

Often, yes — that is the point of phased restoration. The crew seals off the work areas and restores the rest first, so you can serve customers again as soon as safely possible.
