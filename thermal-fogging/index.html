What It Is — How Thermal Fogging Works

Fogging works only after cleaning — it neutralizes odor, it does not remove soot. Here is the full process.

  1. Cleaning comes firstSoot and residue are removed from all surfaces. Fogging over dirty surfaces just seals the odor in.
  2. The home is preparedThe space is sealed, sensitive items are protected, and the home is vacated for the treatment.
  3. Heated fog is appliedA thermal fogger heats the deodorizing solution into a fine mist that behaves like smoke — reaching the same hidden places.
  4. Dwell timeThe fog is left to penetrate porous materials and neutralize odor molecules where they hide.
  5. Ventilation and verificationThe home is aired out and the crew checks every room for remaining odor before handing it back.

Quick Answers

Is thermal fogging safe?

Yes, when done by professionals. The home is vacated during treatment and ventilated afterward. The fogging solution is designed for this exact use — it is not a household spray used at scale.

How long does thermal fogging take?

Most homes take half a day to a full day, including preparation, fogging, dwell time, and ventilation.

Does thermal fogging work on protein smoke odor?

Yes — it is one of the best tools for it. Protein smoke hides in porous materials, and heated fog penetrates those same materials. Heavy protein odor sometimes needs a second pass or a follow-up ozone treatment.

Will the smoke odor come back after fogging?

Not if the soot and residue were properly cleaned first. Fogging neutralizes odor; it does not remove soot. That is why cleaning always comes before fogging in a professional job.

Do I need to leave my home during fogging?

Yes. The home is vacated during treatment and for the airing period afterward. The crew will tell you exactly when it is safe to return.
