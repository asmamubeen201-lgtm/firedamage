The 5 Red Flags

🚩 Red Flag 1 — Full payment demanded upfront

They want the whole job paid — or your full insurance claim check signed over — before any work starts. Legitimate pros work from a written scope with payment tied to completed milestones. Full payment upfront is the single biggest warning sign in restoration.

🚩 Red Flag 2 — No license or insurance proof

They cannot show proof of general liability and workers' compensation insurance, or they dodge the question. Texas does not require a general state contractor license for restoration work — which makes insurance proof even more important, not less. No proof, no hire.

🚩 Red Flag 3 — High-pressure Assignment of Benefits signing

They push you to sign an Assignment of Benefits (AoB) on the spot — a document that hands your insurance claim rights to them. High-pressure AoB signing right after a fire is a classic scam pattern. Never sign an AoB, or any contract you do not fully understand, without getting the scope in writing first and asking your insurer or your state's Department of Insurance.

🚩 Red Flag 4 — No written scope or estimate

They quote a price verbally, promise to "sort the paperwork later," or start work with nothing in writing. A real scope lists every service, the price for each, the expected timeline, and the warranty — before work begins. No written scope means no agreement.

🚩 Red Flag 5 — Door-knockers right after the fire

Someone appears at your door within hours of the fire, sometimes following the fire trucks. These operators — often called storm chasers — pressure fast decisions before you can check credentials. Take their card, verify license and insurance, and compare a written scope before agreeing to anything.

What to Check Before Hiring Anyone

Five minutes of checking saves months of regret. Run through this list before you sign or pay anything:

  • Insurance proof, in writing. General liability and workers' compensation. Ask for the certificate, not a verbal promise.
  • Local permit requirements. Verify what your city requires for structural repair — a real pro knows the answer immediately.
  • Written scope with prices. Every service, every price, the timeline, and the warranty — before work starts.
  • References you can actually call. Recent jobs, ideally in your area, with real homeowners willing to talk.
  • Written warranty. What is covered, for how long, and who honors it — in the contract, not in conversation.
  • No pressure. A pro who respects your timeline is a pro. A pro who rushes you is a salesperson.

Is it normal for a restoration company to ask for full payment upfront?

No. Demanding full payment before work starts is the biggest red flag in restoration. Legitimate pros work from a written scope with payment tied to completed milestones. Never sign over your full claim check or pay everything upfront.

How do I check a fire damage contractor's license and insurance?

Ask for proof of general liability and workers' compensation insurance in writing, and check local city permit requirements for structural repair. Texas has no general state contractor license for restoration, so insurance proof and a written scope matter even more.

What is an Assignment of Benefits and why is it risky?

An Assignment of Benefits hands your insurance claim rights to someone else — usually the contractor. High-pressure AoB signing right after a fire is a red flag. Get the scope in writing first and ask your insurer or your state's Department of Insurance before signing anything.

Someone knocked on my door right after the fire — should I hire them?

Be very cautious. Door-knockers who appear within hours of a fire — sometimes called storm chasers — are a classic scam pattern. They pressure fast decisions before you can check credentials. Take their card, verify license and insurance, and compare a written scope before agreeing to anything.

What should a written restoration scope include?
