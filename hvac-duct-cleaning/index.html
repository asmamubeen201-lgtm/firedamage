What It Is — How HVAC Duct Cleaning Works

Duct cleaning after a fire is a containment job, not a vacuum job. Here is the process.

  1. System is shut downThe HVAC is switched off immediately so it cannot spread more soot while the crew works.
  2. Ducts are inspectedCameras and inspection ports show how far soot traveled and whether the coil and blower are contaminated.
  3. Negative-air cleaningA powerful vacuum creates negative pressure while brushes and air whips loosen soot from duct walls.
  4. Coil and blower are cleanedThe evaporator coil, blower, and drain pan are cleaned — soot here re-contaminates everything downstream.
  5. Filters are replaced and testedNew filters go in, the system is test-run, and the air is checked for remaining smoke odor.

When You Need It

If any of these apply, your duct system is spreading soot — even if the rooms look clean.

  1. Smoke smell when the system runsOdor that appears only when the HVAC kicks on is coming from the ducts.
  2. Soot at vents and registersBlack film around supply or return vents means soot traveled through the system.
  3. The system ran during the fireAny system that was running during the fire pulled smoke straight into the ductwork.
  4. Visible debris in ductsAsh and soot visible at registers means the whole duct run is contaminated.
  5. Dust and soot return after cleaningRooms that get dirty again quickly after cleaning are being re-contaminated by the HVAC.
  6. Allergy or irritation flare-upsFamily members coughing or irritated only indoors may be breathing duct-borne soot particles.

Quick Answers

Why can't I just change the filter?

A new filter catches new particles, but it cannot remove the soot already coating your ducts, coil, and blower. The system will keep blowing that contamination into every room until it is cleaned.

Is duct cleaning enough after a fire?

It is one part of the job. If soot also reached walls, ceilings, and contents, those need cleaning too — otherwise the home still smells of smoke even with clean ducts.

How long does post-fire duct cleaning take?

Most homes take about half a day. Larger homes or multi-system homes can take a full day.

Do I need duct cleaning if the HVAC was off during the fire?

Usually less urgently — but if the fire produced heavy smoke, soot can still enter open returns and vents. A quick inspection will tell you honestly whether cleaning is needed.

Will my insurance cover duct cleaning after a fire?

Duct cleaning is usually covered as part of a fire restoration claim, since the smoke damage is a direct result of the fire. We document the contamination for your adjuster.
