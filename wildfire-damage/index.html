What It Is — How Wildfire Damage Works

Wildfire restoration works from the outside in: exterior first, then smoke, then the land.

  1. Exterior assessmentRoof, vents, gutters, siding, and windows are inspected for ember damage — the small holes that let fire in.
  2. Ember damage repairDamaged roofing, vent screens, and siding are secured and documented for your claim.
  3. Smoke infiltration cleaningInterior surfaces, ducts, and soft contents are cleaned of fine wildfire soot and smoke residue.
  4. Attic and insulation checkAttic insulation is inspected — it absorbs wildfire smoke like a sponge and may need removal.
  5. Defensible-space resetBurned landscaping is cleared and the defensible space around the home is reset to reduce future risk.

When You Need It

If a wildfire came near your home — even if it never touched it — look for these signs.

  1. Ember damage to roof or ventsSmall burn marks on roofing, vent screens, or gutters mean embers reached your home.
  2. Smoke throughout the homeIf the whole house smells of smoke, wildfire smoke infiltrated through vents, windows, and the HVAC.
  3. Ash on every surfaceFine ash on shelves, sills, and floors means wildfire smoke entered the home.
  4. Landscaping burnedBurned plants, fences, and outbuildings are part of the damage — and part of the claim.
  5. Evacuation order liftedOnce authorities clear you to return, the home needs professional assessment before you live in it again.
  6. Melted or warped exterior fixturesMelted gutters, warped siding, or damaged outdoor equipment mean the heat reached your property.

Quick Answers

What is defensible space?

It is the cleared area around your home — typically the first 30 feet — kept free of dense vegetation and flammable material so wildfire cannot reach the structure. After a fire, resetting it is part of the restoration.

Is my home safe to re-enter after a wildfire?

Only after authorities give the all-clear. Once cleared, have the home professionally assessed before living in it — ember damage, smoke, and structural issues are not always visible.

How long does wildfire damage restoration take?

Most homes take 1–4 weeks. Smoke-only jobs are faster; structural damage and landscaping work take longer.

Will insurance cover wildfire damage?

Wildfire damage is usually covered under a standard homeowner policy, including smoke damage, ember damage, and landscaping. Document everything with photos — we provide a full record for your adjuster.

My home never caught fire — do I still need restoration?

Often, yes. Wildfire smoke infiltrates homes through vents, windows, and HVAC systems, and it contaminates insulation, ducts, and soft contents. Smoke-only restoration is a real and common job.
