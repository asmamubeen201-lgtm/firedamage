When You Need Repair — Not Just Cleanup

If any of these apply, your home needs rebuilding, not just cleaning — call before the damage spreads.

  1. Compromised Structural ElementsVisible warping, cracks, or missing framing indicate that rebuilding, not just cleaning, is required.
  2. Damaged Electrical SystemBurned outlets, tripped breakers, or exposed wiring signal the need for full electrical repair.
  3. Roofing FailureCharred shingles, holes, or water intrusion after a fire point to roof reconstruction.
  4. Flooring LossSoot‑blackened or melted flooring that cannot be salvaged requires replacement.
  5. Plumbing DamageBurst pipes, scorched joints, or water‑damage stains on walls suggest plumbing must be rebuilt.
  6. Finish DeteriorationPeeling paint, ruined trim, or destroyed fixtures indicate cosmetic and functional repair is needed.
  7. Insulation and Sheathing DamageDiscolored or crumbling insulation and wall sheathing require removal and replacement before rebuilding.

What It Costs

Fire damage repair typically falls inside the national restoration range of $3,083–$52,031, averaging $27,477 (Angi, Sep 2026). That range covers the whole job from cleanup through rebuild. Before repair starts, the restoration steps that precede it run on their own national ranges — board-up $300–$900, soot removal $1,000–$4,000, fire water extraction $1,000–$3,500, wall and ceiling cleaning $800–$3,000, pack-out and storage $1,000–$5,000.

Your final repair price depends on the scope of the rebuild — how much was removed, what materials are needed, and whether permits are required. Every job starts with a free on-site assessment and a written scope before any work begins.

What drives the price up or down:

  • Extent of structural damage
  • Complexity of electrical and plumbing systems
  • Scope of roofing and flooring replacement

  • Local permit and code requirements

Quick Answers

What is the difference between fire damage repair and restoration?

Repair rebuilds the components removed or destroyed after a fire, while restoration only mitigates, cleans, dries, and removes odors. Repair restores the home’s structural and functional integrity.

How long does fire damage repair usually take?

Repair timelines depend on the extent of structural, electrical, and plumbing work, typically ranging from weeks to months. The process follows assessment, permitting, rebuilding, and final walkthrough phases.

Does insurance cover fire damage repair?

Most policies cover repair costs after the restoration phase, subject to deductible and policy limits. Insurers often reference the national job range of $3,083–$52,031 for total claim estimates.

Are permits required for fire damage repair?

Yes, local building codes usually mandate permits for structural, electrical, and plumbing work. Contractors obtain these permits before reconstruction begins to ensure compliance.

How should I choose a fire damage repair contractor?

Select a licensed contractor with documented fire‑repair experience, clear quoted‑per‑scope estimates, and proper insurance. Verify references and ensure they handle permits and inspections as part of the service.
