What It Is — How Clothing and Textile Cleaning Works

Textile cleaning after a fire is a specialist process. Here is how it works.

  1. Items are sortedClothing and textiles are sorted by fabric type, color, and smoke exposure — with you, so nothing is cleaned without your say-so.
  2. Ozone treatmentOzone gas oxidizes smoke odor molecules trapped in fibers, breaking them apart rather than masking them.
  3. Ultrasonic cleaningDelicate and heavily affected items are cleaned ultrasonically — sound waves lift soot and residue from deep in the fibers.
  4. Laundering and pressingItems are laundered with deodorizing solutions, then pressed and folded.
  5. Return by roomCleaned items come back packed by room and category, using the original inventory.

When You Need It

If your soft furnishings smell of smoke, they need professional textile cleaning — not another wash cycle.

  1. Clothes smell after washingSmoke odor that survives normal washing is trapped in the fibers. It needs ozone or ultrasonic treatment.
  2. Soot on drapes and curtainsDrapes hang in the smoke path and absorb soot across their whole surface.
  3. Upholstery smells of smokeSofas and chairs hold smoke in foam and fabric. Surface cleaning cannot reach it.
  4. Bedding and mattresses affectedMattresses and bedding absorb smoke deeply and are among the hardest items to treat.
  5. A whole wardrobe in the smoke pathWhen a closet was in the smoke path, the entire wardrobe needs treatment, not just the top layer.
  6. Dry-clean-only items exposedSilk, wool, and dry-clean-only fabrics need specialist handling — home washing can ruin them.

Quick Answers

Can smoke smell really be removed from clothes?

Yes. Ozone and ultrasonic cleaning break down smoke odor at the fiber level — normal washing cannot do that, which is why the smell survives the washing machine.

What about dry-clean-only items?

Dry-clean-only fabrics like silk and wool are handled separately with specialist methods. Never home-wash them — water and heat can set the damage permanently.

How long does textile cleaning take?

Typical turnaround is 3–7 days. Larger jobs and heavily affected items take longer.

What can't be saved?

Items that were charred, melted, or soaked through with contaminated water usually cannot be saved. A crew sorts items with you and tells you honestly what is restorable.

Will insurance cover textile cleaning?

Textile cleaning is usually part of a fire restoration contents claim. The pack-out inventory documents every item and its condition for your adjuster.
