What It Is — How Kitchen Fire Damage Works

Kitchen fire restoration follows a fixed order — clean the residue, treat the odor, rebuild what was damaged.

  1. Safety assessmentThe kitchen is checked for structural damage, gas leaks, and live wiring before anyone starts work.
  2. Source removalBurnt food, grease, and charred materials are removed. Odor cannot be treated while its source is still in the room.
  3. Soot and residue cleaningWalls, ceilings, cabinets, and appliances are cleaned with chemistry made for protein smoke.
  4. Odor treatmentThermal fogging or ozone treatment neutralizes odor trapped in cabinets, ducts, and porous materials.
  5. Contents and rebuild-readySalvageable contents are cleaned or packed out, and the kitchen is left ready for repair or rebuild.

When You Need It

Kitchen fires range from a scorched pan to a gutted room. These are the signs yours needs professional restoration.

  1. Grease fire damageGrease fires burn hot and fast, coating everything in sticky protein residue.
  2. Appliance fireOven, stove, toaster, or microwave fires damage the appliance and everything around it.
  3. Smoke through the houseKitchen smoke travels fast through open plans and ducts into living rooms and bedrooms.
  4. Damaged cabinetsHeat warps cabinets and melts finishes. Smoke gets inside closed cabinets and drawers.
  5. Melted fixtures and outletsMelted light fixtures, outlets, and switches mean the fire was hot enough to damage wiring.
  6. A smell that will not leaveProtein smoke odor that survives cleaning is the signature of a kitchen fire — it needs dedicated treatment.

What It Costs

Kitchen fire damage restoration typically costs $2,000–$8,000 nationally. A contained kitchen fire is separately reported at $3,000–$8,000 (Mesa Damage Restoration). The final price depends on how far the fire and smoke spread beyond the kitchen.

What drives the price up or down:

  • Spread — a fire contained to the stove costs far less than one that reached the whole house.
  • Cabinet damage — warped or melted cabinets cost more than cabinets that only need cleaning.
  • Appliance replacement — destroyed appliances are a separate cost from the restoration itself.
  • Odor severity — protein smoke odor needs dedicated treatment and sometimes repeat passes.
  • Structural damage — heat-damaged walls, ceilings, or wiring add repair work.

Quick Answers

How long until I can cook in my kitchen again?

Most contained kitchen fires take 3–10 days to restore. You can cook again once cleaning and odor treatment are complete and the crew clears the kitchen — not before.

Will my kitchen cabinets need replacing?

Usually not. Most cabinets can be cleaned and treated. Replacement is only needed when cabinets are heat-damaged, warped, or waterlogged.

Does insurance cover kitchen fire damage?

Kitchen fire damage is usually covered under a standard homeowner policy, including cleaning, odor removal, and repairs. We document everything for your adjuster.

What about the rest of the house — did the smoke reach it?

Often, yes. Kitchen smoke travels through open plans and ducts. A professional assessment checks the whole home, not just the kitchen, before quoting.

Why is kitchen fire odor so hard to remove?

Because kitchen fires produce protein smoke — an invisible, sticky residue with a smell far worse than its appearance suggests. It needs dedicated chemistry and fogging or ozone treatment, not general cleaning.
