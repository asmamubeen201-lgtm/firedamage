What It Is — How Ozone Treatment Works

Ozone treatment is a controlled process. Every step exists for a reason — skip one and it does not work, or it is unsafe.

  1. Cleaning comes firstSoot and residue are removed. Ozone treats odor, not dirt — cleaning first is non-negotiable.
  2. The space is sealed and vacatedThe treatment area is sealed, and people, pets, and plants are removed. No one stays inside during treatment.
  3. Ozone generators runCommercial ozone generators fill the space with ozone gas, which oxidizes odor molecules on surfaces and in the air.
  4. Airing period followsAfter treatment, the space is ventilated until ozone levels are back to normal and the home is safe to re-enter.
  5. Odor is verifiedThe crew checks every room. If odor remains in deep materials, a second treatment or thermal fogging is scheduled.

Quick Answers

Is ozone treatment safe?

Yes, when done by professionals. The space is sealed and unoccupied during treatment, and the home is ventilated until ozone levels are back to normal before anyone returns. Ozone is powerful — that is why it is pro-only.

How long before I can re-enter my home?

Most treatments take 1–2 days including the airing period. The crew will tell you exactly when it is safe to return — never re-enter early.

Ozone vs thermal fogging — which is better?

They work differently. Fogging penetrates porous materials with a deodorizing mist; ozone oxidizes odor molecules in the air and on surfaces. Many tough jobs need both, which is why crews often combine them.

Can ozone damage anything in my home?

Ozone can damage some rubber and latex items, which is why professionals remove or protect them before treatment. Plants and pets are also removed. Your crew will walk you through what needs protecting.

Will the smoke odor come back after ozone treatment?

Not if the soot and residue were properly cleaned first. Ozone destroys odor molecules, but it cannot remove soot — cleaning always comes before treatment in a professional job.
