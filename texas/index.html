Texas Fire Insurance Claim Playbook

Curated from Texas Department of Insurance guidance. Verify current rules before filing.

  1. Most Texas HO-3 homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental fire damage, including smoke and water damage from firefighting.
  2. You have the right to choose your own restoration contractor — your insurer cannot force you to use their preferred vendor.
  3. Document everything before cleanup begins: photos/video of every room, list of damaged contents.
  4. Most policies require prompt notice — call your insurer within 24–48 hours of the fire.
  5. Keep receipts for emergency board-up, temporary housing, and meals — Additional Living Expenses (ALE) coverage usually reimburses these.
  6. Do not sign over your full claim check or sign an Assignment of Benefits without understanding it — get the scope in writing first.

Quick Answers — Fire Damage in Texas

How much does fire damage restoration cost in Texas?

Most Texas jobs fall between $3,083 and $52,031 nationally (Angi, Sep 2026), averaging $27,477. Board-up runs $300–$900, soot removal $1,000–$4,000, and a contained kitchen fire $2,000–$8,000.

Does Texas law let me choose my own restoration contractor?

Yes. You have the right to choose your own contractor — your insurer cannot force you to use their preferred vendor. Verify current rules with the Texas Department of Insurance.

How fast is emergency response in Texas?

Board-up and securing are usually same-day, 2–4 hours on site. Full restoration timelines depend on damage scope — most homes take days to weeks.

What should I do in the first 48 hours after a fire in Texas?

Call 911 if the fire is active. Do not re-enter or run the HVAC. Photograph everything, call your insurer within 24–48 hours, and get emergency board-up started — your policy requires you to mitigate further damage.
