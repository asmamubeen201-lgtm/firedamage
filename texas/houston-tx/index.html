7 Signs You Need Fire Damage Restoration in Houston

Fire damage is not always visible flames. These are the surface signals that appear as smoke, soot and firefighting water do their quiet damage — often hours after the fire is out.

  1. Smoke smell that will not air out — if the smell is still there the next morning, it has bonded into walls, ducts and soft contents.
  2. Soot on walls or ceilings — black or brown film, especially around outlets, vents and ceiling corners.
  3. Yellow or brown stains on walls — smoke residue bleeding through paint, common after kitchen fires.
  4. Warped floors or swollen baseboards — water from firefighting soaks in fast and starts mold within 24–48 hours.
  5. Outlets that smell burnt — electrical fire residue in the walls; do not use those outlets.
  6. HVAC blowing smoky air — the system ran during or after the fire and is now re-circulating soot through the whole house.
  7. Peeling paint or blistered surfaces — heat damage under the surface, often worse in humid Houston homes where moisture was already present.

What To Do Right Now (Before We Arrive)

  • If the fire is still active, call 911 first — do not re-enter the home.
  • Do not turn on the HVAC — it spreads soot through every room.

The Houston Fire Factor: Why Fires Hit Different Here

Houston's risk is a combination of age and climate. Inside the Loop, The Heights and Montrose are full of 1920s bungalows with original wiring and pier-and-beam foundations — electrical fires in pre-1970 homes are one of the city's leading causes. Outside the Loop, 1970s–90s suburbs like Clear Lake and the Energy Corridor face a different problem: spring lightning storms and garage/workshop fires.

Then there is the humidity. In a dry city, soot sits on surfaces. In Houston, moisture helps it bond into drywall, insulation and soft contents — which is why Houston soot jobs so often include dehumidification and HVAC duct cleaning in the same scope. And every Houstonian knows the rhythm: Rodeo season in February and March, hurricane season after that. Preparedness is a way of life here — fire response should be too.

Quick Answers — Fire Damage in Houston

How much does fire damage restoration cost in Houston?

Most Houston jobs fall between $3,083 and $52,031 nationally (Angi, Sep 2026), with a national average of $27,477. In Houston, board-up runs $300–$900, soot removal $1,000–$4,000, and a contained kitchen fire $2,000–$8,000. The final price depends on the size of the home and how far smoke and water spread.

Will homeowner's insurance cover fire damage in Texas?

Most Texas HO-3 homeowner policies cover sudden and accidental fire damage, including smoke and water damage from firefighting. You choose your own restoration contractor — your insurer cannot force you to use their preferred vendor. Call your insurer within 24–48 hours and document everything before cleanup begins.

How fast can you board up my Houston home?

Same-day in most cases. Board-up crews cover windows, doors and openings within hours of the call — usually 2–4 hours on site. In Houston, humidity makes soot set faster, so securing the property quickly also protects what is left inside.

How long does smoke odor removal take in Houston?

Thermal fogging or ozone treatment usually takes half a day to two days, depending on how far smoke traveled. Houston humidity can make odor bond harder into soft contents, so duct cleaning is often part of the same job. Your HVAC must not run until the ducts are cleaned.

Do I need a permit for fire restoration in Houston?

Structural repair work in Houston generally requires a city permit; emergency board-up and cleaning do not. Your restoration contractor should handle permits for rebuild work. Confirm the contractor carries general liability and workers comp insurance before work starts.

Why is soot worse in Houston homes?

Two reasons. First, the Inner Loop — The Heights, Montrose, Meyerland — has many pre-1970 homes with original wiring, a leading electrical-fire cause. Second, Houston humidity lets soot embed deeper into walls and soft contents, which is why soot cleanup here often pairs with dehumidification and duct cleaning.
