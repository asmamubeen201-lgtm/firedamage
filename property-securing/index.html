What It Is — How Property Securing Works

Securing is a full perimeter job, not just a few boards. Here is how it works.

  1. Full perimeter walkThe crew walks the entire property line and building exterior, noting every opening, weak fence section, and access point.
  2. Openings are boardedWindows, doors, and wall openings are covered with plywood or steel panels.
  3. Fencing goes upTemporary fencing closes gaps in the yard perimeter so the property itself is enclosed.
  4. Locks are changedDamaged or compromised locks are replaced so old keys no longer work.
  5. Shutoffs are coordinatedGas, water, and power are confirmed safe or shut off as needed, and the crew documents everything for your insurer.

When You Need It

If the home will be empty for any length of time, it needs securing — not just board-up.

  1. The home will be vacantAny fire-damaged home that cannot be lived in is a target for theft and vandalism.
  2. Valuables remain insideFurniture, electronics, and tools left inside need a locked property, not just a locked door.
  3. Fencing is damaged or missingA broken fence line means anyone can walk onto the property unseen.
  4. Locks were forced or meltedFirefighters force doors; heat destroys locks. Old keys may still work — or no lock may work at all.
  5. Your insurer requires mitigationPolicies require reasonable steps to prevent further damage and loss. Securing is that step.
  6. The neighborhood has had problemsIf break-ins happen nearby, an empty fire-damaged home is the easiest target on the street.

Quick Answers

What is the difference between board-up and property securing?

Board-up covers the openings — windows and doors. Property securing covers the whole property: board-up plus fencing, lock changes, and shutoff coordination. Securing is the complete package for a home that will sit empty.

Does insurance pay for property securing?

Securing is usually covered as emergency mitigation under a standard homeowner policy, because it prevents theft and further damage. We document everything — photos and an itemized list — for your claim.

How fast can a property be secured?

Crews dispatch 24/7 and most securing jobs are completed the same day, typically within a few hours on site.

Should the locks really be changed after a fire?

Yes. Firefighters force doors, heat damages lock mechanisms, and in the chaos keys go missing. New locks mean only you — and people you authorize — can get in.

What happens to the fencing when restoration starts?

Temporary fencing stays up until the property is safe and occupied again. It is removed as part of the restoration, once permanent fencing and doors are back in place.
