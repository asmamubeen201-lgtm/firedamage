What It Is — How Electronics Cleaning Works

Electronics cleaning is delicate, hands-on work. Here is the professional process.

  1. Power off immediatelyDevices are unplugged and batteries removed. Never test a smoke-exposed device by switching it on.
  2. AssessmentEach item is opened and inspected — soot inside the case matters far more than soot on the outside.
  3. DecontaminationCircuit boards and contacts are cleaned with electronics-safe solutions that remove acidic soot without damaging components.
  4. DryingItems are dried thoroughly in a controlled environment. Moisture plus soot is what accelerates corrosion.
  5. TestingDevices are powered up and tested only after cleaning and drying are complete.

When You Need It

If your electronics were in the smoke path, assume they need cleaning — even if they look clean.

  1. Devices were on during the fireRunning electronics pulled smoke through their fans and vents, straight onto circuit boards.
  2. Soot inside vents or fansBlack film in a vent or fan grill means soot is inside the device too.
  3. Smoke-exposed TVs and computersScreens and computers are the most expensive items in most homes — and the most vulnerable to soot.
  4. Error or failure after the fireA device that worked before the fire and fails after it is almost certainly a soot victim.
  5. A sharp smell from the deviceIf a device smells of smoke when it runs, soot is heating up on its components.
  6. Appliances in the fire roomWashers, dryers, and kitchen appliances in or near the fire room need inspection before use.

Quick Answers

Should I turn on a smoke-exposed device to test it?

No. Switching on a soot-contaminated device can short-circuit it and turn a cleanable item into a dead one. Keep it powered off until it has been professionally cleaned.

Can smoke really damage electronics?

Yes. Soot is acidic and conductive. It corrodes circuit boards and contacts, and the damage often shows up weeks after the fire — a device that seems fine today can fail next month.

How long does electronics cleaning take?

It varies by item count — a few devices can be done in a day; a whole home's electronics take longer. Testing is included in the timeline.

Is it worth cleaning electronics, or should I just replace them?

It depends on the item's value and exposure. High-value items — televisions, computers, audio gear — are usually worth cleaning. Cheap items often are not. A crew will tell you honestly, item by item.

Will insurance cover electronics cleaning?

Electronics cleaning is usually part of a fire restoration contents claim. Keep the inventory from your pack-out — it documents every item and its condition for your adjuster.
