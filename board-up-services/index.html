What It Is — How Board-Up Services Works

Board-up is the first call after a fire. Here is exactly what happens from the moment you call.

  1. Call and describe the openingsTell us which windows, doors, or walls are open. We dispatch the nearest crew and give you an arrival window.
  2. Damage is photographed firstThe crew photographs every opening before touching anything, so your insurance claim has a clear record.
  3. Panels are cut and fittedPlywood or steel panels are measured and cut to fit each opening, then secured with tamper-resistant fasteners.
  4. Every entry point is checkedRoof vents, garage doors, skylights, and side doors are inspected — not just the obvious broken windows.
  5. You get a written recordA list of everything boarded, with photos, for your insurer. No surprises, no guesswork.

When You Need It

If any of these apply, your home needs securing tonight — not tomorrow.

  1. Broken or missing windowsFire or firefighting broke the glass, or windows were vented by the fire crew.
  2. Doors that will not close or lockHeat warps frames, and forced entry by firefighters can destroy locks and hinges.
  3. Holes in walls or roofFire crews cut openings to reach the fire. Rain through those holes destroys what the fire did not.
  4. You cannot stay in the homeIf the home is uninhabitable, it is also unguarded. Board-up is your lock and key.
  5. Rain is forecast before repairs startOne storm through an open roof can add thousands in water damage on top of the fire damage.
  6. Your insurer asked you to mitigateMost policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Board-up is that step.
  7. The home will sit empty for daysVacant fire-damaged homes attract theft and vandalism fast. Secure it the same day.

What It Costs

Board-up typically costs $300–$900 nationally. Published per-window rates run $150–$400, and multi-opening jobs typically run $400–$1,200 (DISS Restoration). Your final price depends on how many openings need covering and what material is used.

What drives the price up or down:

  • Number of openings — each window or door adds material and labor.
  • Plywood vs steel — steel costs more but is far harder to defeat on a vacant property.
  • Height and access — second-story and roof-level openings take longer and cost more.
  • Emergency timing — night and weekend dispatch can add to the bill, but waiting costs more.
  • Property condition — unstable walls or roof sections slow the crew down.

Quick Answers

How fast can board-up happen after a fire?

Crews dispatch 24/7 and typically arrive the same day; most jobs take 2–4 hours on site. If the fire department is still on scene, we coordinate with them and start as soon as the property is released.

Does homeowner's insurance pay for board-up?

Board-up is usually covered as emergency mitigation under a standard homeowner policy, because policies require you to prevent further damage. Keep every receipt — we photograph and document everything for your claim.

Can I board up the windows myself?

You can, but it is risky: fire-damaged walls and roofs can be unstable, and live wiring may be exposed. Professional crews also give your insurer the documentation a DIY job cannot.

How long can the boards stay up?

Boards are temporary protection and can stay up until permanent repairs begin — days or weeks. They are removed as part of the restoration, not left as a permanent fix.

What if it rains before the crew arrives?

Tell us when you call. Crews carry heavy-duty tarps and prioritize roof and ceiling openings first, so rain cannot reach the interior while the boards go up.
