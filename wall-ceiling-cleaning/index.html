What It Is — How Wall and Ceiling Cleaning Works

Walls and ceilings are cleaned in a fixed order — skip a step and the stain comes back through the paint.

  1. Test cleaning comes firstA small hidden area is tested to identify the soot type and the right cleaning chemistry.
  2. Dry soot is liftedDry chemical sponges remove loose soot without smearing it into the paint.
  3. Wet cleaning followsThe correct solution for the soot type is washed across walls and ceilings, section by section.
  4. Surfaces are deodorizedOdor treatment neutralizes the smoke smell trapped in porous paint and drywall.
  5. Sealed and ready for paintA smoke-sealing primer locks in any remaining residue so the new paint stays clean and odor-free.

When You Need It

Smoke damage on walls and ceilings is often worse than it looks. These are the signs.

  1. Yellow or brown stainingSmoke discolors paint, especially on ceilings where hot smoke collects.
  2. Dark lines above doors and windowsSmoke follows air currents — the lines above openings are its path.
  3. Ghosting on wallsFaint outlines of studs and framing showing through are smoke residue settling unevenly.
  4. Stains that return after paintingIf a stain bled through a previous paint job, the surface was never sealed. It needs professional cleaning and primer.
  5. A smell that survives cleaningOdor trapped in paint and drywall means the residue is still there, even if the wall looks clean.
  6. Soot on ceiling fixturesBlackened light fixtures and ceiling fans mean smoke reached the ceiling across the room.

Quick Answers

Can smoke-stained walls be saved, or do they need replacing?

Most smoke-stained walls can be saved. Drywall is usually cleaned, sealed, and repainted. Replacement is only needed when the drywall itself is heat-damaged, waterlogged, or structurally compromised.

Why does the smoke stain come back through new paint?

Because the surface was painted without a smoke-sealing primer. Soot residue bleeds through regular paint. The fix is proper cleaning first, then a sealing primer, then paint.

How long does wall and ceiling cleaning take?

Most homes take 1–2 days. A single room can be done in a day; a whole house with heavy staining takes longer.

How much does it cost per room?

Smoke damage cleanup is reported at $200–$1,200 per room (Angi, Jul 2026), depending on stain severity and ceiling height. Whole-home jobs are quoted as a package.

Will the smoke smell go away after cleaning?

Yes, when cleaning is followed by deodorization and sealing. Cleaning alone often leaves odor trapped in porous paint — that is why the sealing step matters.
