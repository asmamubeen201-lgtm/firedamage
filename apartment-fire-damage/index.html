What It Is — How Apartment Fire Damage Works

Apartment restoration is coordination as much as cleaning. Here is how a professional crew handles it.

  1. Your unit is assessedDamage to your unit is documented — structure, contents, and smoke spread — with photos for every party involved.
  2. Common areas are checkedHallways, stairwells, and shared systems are inspected. Smoke and water travel through buildings fast.
  3. Landlord and HOA are coordinatedThe crew works with the building owner or HOA on scope, access, and who is responsible for what.
  4. Contents are cleaned or packed outYour belongings are cleaned on site or packed out for off-site cleaning and storage.
  5. Documentation for all partiesSeparate, clear records go to you, your landlord, and your insurer — so nobody has to argue about what happened.

When You Need It

Whether the fire started in your unit or your neighbor's, these are the signs you need professional help.

  1. Fire in your unitAny fire in your own apartment needs professional cleaning and restoration — even a small one.
  2. Smoke from a neighbor's unitSmoke travels through shared walls, ducts, and hallways. Your unit can be damaged by a fire you never saw.
  3. Building evacuationIf the building was evacuated, the damage is serious enough to need professional assessment before you move back in.
  4. Landlord asking for documentationWhen your landlord wants a record of the damage, a professional assessment protects you.
  5. Common-area damageSmoke or water in hallways and stairwells means the building systems were affected, not just your unit.
  6. A smell that will not leaveSmoke odor in an apartment comes from shared ducts and walls — it needs professional treatment, not air freshener.

What It Costs

Apartment fire damage restoration typically costs $1,500–$6,000 nationally. The final price varies by building management — how many units are involved, who is responsible for what, and how the building is managed.

What drives the price up or down:

  • Units involved — one unit costs far less than several units plus common areas.
  • Building management — HOA and landlord coordination adds time and paperwork.
  • Common-area damage — hallways, stairwells, and shared systems add scope.
  • Contents volume — more belongings means more cleaning or pack-out.
  • Smoke spread — smoke through shared ducts affects more units than the fire itself.

Quick Answers

Who pays for apartment fire damage — the tenant or the landlord?

It varies by lease and by state. Usually the building owner's policy covers the structure and common areas, while your renter's insurance covers your belongings. Check your lease and both policies — we document for both sides so the claim is clear.

Can I stay in my apartment during restoration?

It depends on the damage. Small, contained fires often allow you to stay; whole-unit restoration with loud equipment and odor treatment usually means staying elsewhere for a few days.

How long does apartment fire restoration take?

It varies by building management — a single unit can take days; multi-unit and common-area work takes longer. You will get a timeline up front, before work starts.

What if my neighbor caused the fire?

Your claim is usually with your own insurer first, regardless of who caused the fire. Our documentation — photos, inventory, and scope — supports any claim, including against a neighbor's policy.

What should I tell my landlord first?

Report the fire immediately and in writing, and do not start cleaning before the damage is documented. A professional assessment protects both you and your landlord.
