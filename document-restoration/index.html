When You Need It

If any of these apply, your documents need professional restoration — not a hair dryer and hope.

  1. Wet or soaked documentsWater from firefighting soaks paper fast. Wet paper tears, sticks, and grows mold within days.
  2. Charred or heat-damaged papersHeat-damaged paper is brittle and fragile. Handling it without training destroys it.
  3. Smoke-damaged photosPhotos are the most commonly lost item in a fire — and the most commonly saved by professionals.
  4. Water-soaked booksBooks swell and warp when wet. Freeze-drying can save many of them if done quickly.
  5. Vital records affectedBirth certificates, deeds, wills, and legal papers need priority treatment and careful documentation.
  6. Ink running or pages stickingRunning ink and stuck pages mean the documents are actively deteriorating. Freeze them, do not peel them apart.

Quick Answers

Can wet documents really be saved?

Often, yes — if they are stabilized quickly. Vacuum freeze-drying removes water without further damage to paper and ink. The key is speed: wet documents deteriorate fast.

How fast must documents be treated?

As fast as possible. Wet paper sticks together, ink runs, and mold starts within days. Freeze or freeze-dry wet documents immediately — do not let them sit in a pile.

What about photos stuck together?

Do not peel them apart — you will tear the images. Professionals separate stuck photos with controlled humidity and specialized methods.

Can documents be digitized as part of restoration?

Yes. Where possible, restored documents and photos are scanned as a backup, so the information survives even if the originals do not.

Will insurance cover document restoration?

Document restoration is usually part of a fire restoration contents claim. Keep the inventory and all receipts — we document every item for your adjuster.
